Reactor dark photons

The useful result is not one exclusion curve. It is a map of three different searches.

What exists

  • Visible dark photon: reactor γ rays convert through γe→A′e; the detector looks for A′e→γe. TEXONO provides the main reusable data. Published limits are recasts.
  • Invisible decay: A′→χχ; NEON looks for χ–electron scattering from 1 to 10 keV using reactor-on minus reactor-off data. This is a direct collaboration result.
  • Nuclear lines: neutron capture can emit A′ in place of a γ. A 2026 TEXONO recast claims reach from 0.1 to 6.9 MeV.
Do not repeat the original NEOS bound without the caveat. The 2019 paper argues that detector stability and selection efficiency do not support Park’s NEOS treatment. The 2024 large multiple-scattering improvement is also disputed by a 2025 GEANT study, which finds less than a 30% change in the mixing limit.

Best next analysis

  1. Get a collaboration-level reactor-on/off spectrum and covariance.
  2. Model the actual reactor γ source and transport, not only the FRJ-1 exponential fit.
  3. Publish signal acceptance for single-site scattering and three-hit e⁺e⁻ decay.
  4. Fit visible, invisible, and dark-axion-portal models separately.
