Core findings

“Dark photon” does not name one experimental target. It covers a hidden U(1) gauge boson whose observable behavior changes with its mass, mass-generation mechanism, dark-sector spectrum, production history, and kinetic mixing with the ordinary photon. A single exclusion curve is therefore meaningful only after its assumptions are stated.

  • The minimal massive model has two portal parameters, dark-photon mass mA′ and kinetic mixing ε. Invisible decays add at least a dark-matter mass mχ and dark coupling αD.
  • The main theory split is massless versus massive. Massive models split again by dark-Higgs versus Stückelberg mass generation and by visible, invisible, or cosmologically stable decays.2,3
  • The search landscape spans at least 20 orders of magnitude in mass: ultralight wave dark matter below 10−12 eV, resonant radio/microwave searches around μeV, stellar and direct-absorption probes through eV–keV, and accelerator searches from MeV to tens of GeV.
  • Mass is not beam energy. A 20 MeV dark photon can be produced with a 100 GeV electron beam or in 13.6 TeV proton collisions. Mass fixes thresholds and lifetime; laboratory energy fixes boost, acceptance, and decay location.
  • Representative null results reach ε≈10−16 at isolated μeV masses for dark-photon dark matter, ε≈10−12–10−9 in selected ultralight laboratory bands, ε≈10−5–10−4 for long-lived MeV dark photons, and ε≈10−4–10−3 for prompt visible MeV–GeV searches. These numbers cannot be compared without matching the underlying assumptions.
  • No accepted discovery exists. Published results are exclusions or projections. The minimal dark-photon explanation of the muon anomalous magnetic moment is strongly constrained by complementary visible and invisible searches.12,13,15
Scope. Literature checked through 11 September 2026. The report prioritizes the canonical kinetically mixed vector portal. Gauged B−L, Lμ−Lτ, baryonic vectors, mirror photons, and composite dark vectors are adjacent models, not interchangeable with the minimal electromagnetic-current dark photon.

Definitions and parameters

A compact low-energy Lagrangian for the canonical massive dark photon is

ℒ ⊃ −¼FμνFμν − ¼F′μνF′μν − (ε/2)FμνF′μν + ½mA′2A′μA′μ + eJEMμAμ.

After diagonalizing the kinetic terms, a massive A′ couples approximately as εeA′μJEMμ. Holdom identified the renormalizable mixing between two Abelian gauge fields and its connection to shifted effective charges.1 The coupling can arise from heavy particles charged under both U(1) groups. Loop expectations are model dependent; very small ε is technically possible.

Mass generation

A dark Higgs gives A′ a mass through spontaneous symmetry breaking and predicts at least one additional scalar. A Stückelberg construction can generate a gauge-invariant mass without a light radial Higgs in the low-energy spectrum. Both lead to a massive vector portal, but their ultraviolet consistency and early-Universe behavior can differ.3

Decay regimes

The electron-pair threshold at 2me≈1.022 MeV is a structural boundary. Above it, visible A′→e+e− is allowed. Muon and hadron channels open at higher masses. If mA′>2mχ and the dark coupling is large enough, invisible decay can dominate. Below the e+e− threshold, a minimal A′ is extremely long-lived and stellar, cosmological, oscillation, or absorption probes become central.2

How to read sensitivity and exclusion limits

  1. Check the model. A visible A′ limit usually assumes no open dark decay. An invisible limit requires a branching-ratio assumption. A DPDM limit assumes A′ is a specified fraction of local dark matter.
  2. Check the axes. ε, ε2, χ, and y appear in different plots. Taking a square root or applying a benchmark transformation can change the visual comparison by orders of magnitude.
  3. Check confidence level. Results commonly quote 90% or 95% CL. Expected sensitivity is not observed exclusion.
  4. Check lifetime and boost. For visible A′ decays, production often scales as ε2, while decay length grows roughly as 1/(ε2mA′) away from thresholds. Acceptance can therefore form a band rather than a simple upper limit.
  5. Check medium effects. Stellar and supernova production depends on plasma mixing, longitudinal modes, opacity, and thermal profiles. SN1987A bounds carry significant modeling uncertainty.9–11
  6. Check halo and polarization assumptions. Cavity and antenna limits depend on local density and the projection of the dark electric field onto the apparatus. Random and fixed polarization cases need separate treatment.
  7. Check theory consistency. A Proca field used as an effective description does not by itself specify a viable early-Universe production mechanism or ultraviolet completion.3

Evidence gaps

  • There is no universal, assumption-free exclusion envelope across the entire mass range.
  • Ultralight direct searches often cover narrow frequency bands with exceptional ε reach; broad coverage remains incomplete.
  • Supernova bounds remain less controlled than laboratory limits because the source is unique and the core microphysics is uncertain.
  • Invisible thermal-target comparisons should be regenerated for the exact χ spin, mass ratio, αD, and cosmological history used in a project.
  • Projected facilities must be labeled separately from operating experiments and published null results.
