Dark-photon searches at reactor-neutrino experiments

Energy ranges, production mechanisms, experimental strategies, reported exclusions, projected sensitivities, and the disputes that change how the limits should be read.

Answer in one screen

Standard visible-vector channelMeV reactor γ rays produce an on-shell dark photon through γe → A′e or γ–A′ conversion in matter. A nearby detector searches for A′e → γe. The rate scales approximately as ε⁴: ε² at production and ε² at detection.
Established analysis windowTEXONO used electron/γ data in 3–8 MeV. Park also recast NEOS data in 1–5 MeV. The useful incident reactor-photon range is roughly 0.2–15 MeV across the studies, but the detector threshold fixes the practical reach.
Mass reachThe conventional reactor Compton-like program mainly targets sub-MeV A′. A 2026 neutron-capture de-excitation proposal extends the recast to 0.1–6.9 MeV using discrete nuclear lines and visible A′ → e⁺e⁻ decays above threshold.
Evidence statusMost visible-A′ reactor limits are phenomenological recasts, not dedicated collaboration searches. NEON is the direct collaboration result, but it searches for light dark matter from invisible A′ decay and is model-dependent.
Do not quote the original NEOS number as settled. Park reported ε < 1.3×10−5 below 1 MeV, but the 2019 reanalysis argued that the necessary detector/background stability was not demonstrated and did not use NEOS. The same reanalysis also restored in-medium oscillations and found loss of sensitivity below roughly 0.1 eV.

Experimental strategy that would settle the issue

  1. Use reactor-on/off data with identical cuts and exposure-normalized spectra. Do not infer a limit from the square root of a huge on-only background count.
  2. Publish the single-site acceptance for A′e→γe and the multi-site acceptance for A′→e⁺e⁻. TEXONO’s anti-Compton selection is signal-dependent.
  3. Build the primary γ spectrum and transport model for the actual core, fuel cycle, moderator, shielding, and detector line of sight. The old FRJ-1 parameterization is useful but not reactor-specific.
  4. Fit energy shape and normalization jointly. For nuclear de-excitation, target line energies; for invisible decay, exploit the reactor-on minus reactor-off keV spectrum.
  5. Report separate likelihoods for visible A′, invisible A′→χχ, and dark-axion-portal models. Their couplings and branching ratios are not interchangeable.

What was checked

The seed PDF was read in full. Seven later primary papers were traced from citations and related-paper searches, downloaded from arXiv, converted to text, and checked at the abstract, setup, results, and conclusion sections. SHA-256 digests and canonical URLs are in the manifest.

Not checked: no exclusion curve was digitized or numerically reproduced; no collaboration likelihood was rerun; the 2026 nuclear-transition calculation was not independently recomputed; the old FRJ-1 reactor γ spectrum was not validated against a modern reactor simulation. Every numerical result above is therefore a sourced claim, not an independent reproduction.
